Your back aches, your belly is growing, and you are not sure what is safe to try. That is a very common spot to be in.

This guide covers why pregnancy strains your back, the warning signs to know first, what helps in each trimester, and how hands-on care fits in.

## Why Pregnancy Causes Back Pain

Back pain in pregnancy has a few overlapping causes. Knowing yours helps you pick the right fix.

### How Common It Is

You are not alone. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/symptoms/back-pain-during-pregnancy) says about 40% of pregnant women have back pain at some point. [Scripps Health](https://www.scripps.org/news_items/2936-how-to-relieve-back-pain-during-pregnancy-8-expert-tips) says up to 70% feel some pain, especially in the lower back.

Other sources report figures between those. The honest answer is that it affects a large share of pregnancies, and the exact number varies by study.

### Hormones, Posture and Weight

The usual contributors are:

- **Hormone changes** that loosen ligaments around the spine and pelvis
- Added weight carried in front of your body
- A shifting center of gravity that changes your posture
- Stretched abdominal muscles that offer less support for your trunk

Cleveland Clinic notes the abdominal muscle change. Your back often works harder to make up for it.

### Lower Back Pain Vs. Pelvic Girdle Pain

Not all pregnancy back pain is the same. Lumbar pain sits across the lower back, around the beltline. Pelvic girdle pain sits lower, near the tailbone or over the back of the pelvis, and often shows up when you roll over, climb stairs or stand on one leg.

Pain that runs down the leg may be sciatica-type pain. If you are unsure which you have, describe exactly where it hurts and what triggers it when you talk to your provider.

## When to Call Your Doctor First

Most pregnancy back pain is a muscle and joint issue. Some is not. Know the difference before you try anything at home.

### Red Flags

Call your ob-gyn or midwife right away if back pain comes with any of these:

- Regular contractions or tightening in your belly
- Fluid leaking or vaginal bleeding
- Pelvic pressure or pain
- Fever, or burning with urination
- Numbness, weakness in a leg, or loss of bladder control
- Severe pain that does not ease

[Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/4498-premature-labor) lists back pain as one possible sign of preterm labor, which is labor before 37 weeks. Preterm labor back pain is usually low, may come in waves, and does not ease when you change position.

### Pain That Lasts

Pain that is severe, or that has lasted more than two weeks, deserves a call to your obstetric provider. Do not wait for it to pass. If you think you may be in early labor, call right away.

## Back Pain Relief During Pregnancy by Trimester

Pregnancy is not one block of time. What your back needs shifts as your body changes. [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/back-pain-during-pregnancy) says that without complications, staying active reduces back pain, and that you should talk with your ob-gyn before starting or changing an exercise program.

### First Trimester

Back pain can start early, even before your belly shows. One hospital source says it can begin in the [first trimester](https://www.gleneagleshospitals.co.in/blogs/obstetrics-gynaecology/back-pain-during-pregnancy-causes-relief-tips-and-safe-remedies), likely tied to hormone changes.

This is a good time to build habits. Keep walking, start gentle core and hip work, and fix your desk setup before your body changes more.

### Second Trimester

Back pain is commonly reported in the second and third trimesters. Your belly is growing and your posture is changing.

Focus on glute and upper back strength, hip mobility, and short movement breaks. Choose supportive shoes and start practicing side-lying sleep.

### Third Trimester

Load is at its highest now. Pelvic girdle pain is more common, and lying flat on your back may feel uncomfortable.

[Banner Health](https://www.bannerhealth.com/healthcareblog/better-me/tips-for-minimizing-back-pain-during-pregnancy) advises avoiding exercises that require lying flat on your back later in pregnancy. Shorter sessions, more rest between sets and more support pillows all help.

## Everyday Fixes That Add Up

Small changes repeated all day often matter more than any single treatment.

### Sleep Position and Pillows

ACOG advises sleeping on your side later in pregnancy and placing a small pillow behind your lower back. A pillow between your knees can also take pressure off your hips.

### Sitting, Standing and Lifting

- Change position every 30 minutes or so if you sit at a desk
- Keep your feet flat and your lower back supported
- Lift by squatting, not bending at the waist, as ACOG advises
- Hold a toddler or a bag close to your body

If you parent a young child, you may not be able to avoid lifting. Bend your knees, exhale as you stand, and ask for help with heavier loads.

### Shoes and Support Belts

ACOG says to avoid high heels. Choose shoes with good cushioning and stable support.

Support belts are a personal call. Some people find them helpful on long days. They should not replace strengthening, and you should check with your provider before relying on one.

### Heat and Cold

Heat and cold are both commonly suggested. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/pregnancy-week-by-week/in-depth/pregnancy/art-20046080) lists them among the first things to try. Use a warm, not hot, pack for short periods and keep a cloth between it and your skin.

## Safe Exercises and Stretches

Movement is the strongest option we have. A 2023 review cited by [Hinge Health](https://www.hingehealth.com/resources/articles/back-pain-during-pregnancy/) found that regular exercise helps manage low back pain in pregnancy and improves everyday function.

### Walking, Swimming and Yoga

Walking, swimming and prenatal yoga are common choices. Water takes weight off your spine, and walking keeps your hips and back moving.

### Strength and Pelvic Tilts

Gentle strengthening can ease the load on your spine. Good starting points are:

1. **Pelvic tilts** on hands and knees or standing against a wall
2. **Cat-cow** for gentle spine movement
3. Side-lying hip abduction for your glutes
4. Supported squats to a chair, matching how you lift

Keep your breathing steady and your range comfortable. If you lifted before pregnancy, ask your provider how to adjust loads, rest and positions.

### What to Avoid

Exercise should not cause pain. ACOG says to stop if it does. Banner Health also advises against pushing through pain and against bed rest unless your provider says you need it.

## Hands-On and Professional Care

Exercise has stronger support than any hands-on option. Still, many people want extra help, and that is reasonable.

### Physical Therapy

Mayo Clinic says physical therapy may help if heat, cold, massage and exercise are not enough. A therapist who treats pregnant patients can build a plan around your trimester and your pain type.

### Massage and Acupuncture

Mayo Clinic says massage is not proven to ease low back pain for long, but it may help you feel better. It also says some research suggests acupuncture might help.

### Chiropractic Care: What the Evidence Says

The evidence is limited. A 2020 systematic review of 50 articles, [as summarized by Dodd Chiropractic](https://doddchiropracticclinic.com/blog/chiropractic-care-during-pregnancy-what-is-safe-and-what-actually-helps), found "inconclusive but favorable" evidence for chiropractic care for pregnancy-related low back pain. Evidence for spinal manipulation specifically stayed inconclusive.

[Healthline](https://healthline.com/health/pregnancy/chiropractor-while-pregnant) says chiropractic care is believed to be safe and advises getting your doctor's approval first. Be wary of any page that promises it carries no risk. Care should complement your prenatal care, not replace it.

If you try it, ask these questions:

- Do you have training in prenatal care?
- Will you coordinate with my ob-gyn?
- How will you position me, especially later in pregnancy?
- What will you do if something does not feel right?

## Medication

Medication is a conversation for your provider, not a default. Acetaminophen (Tylenol) appears as an over-the-counter option on [Banner Health](https://www.bannerhealth.com/healthcareblog/better-me/tips-for-minimizing-back-pain-during-pregnancy) and Hinge Health. Guidance can change, so confirm the current advice with your ob-gyn before taking anything.

Bring these questions: Which pain reliever is okay for me, at what dose, and for how long? What should I do if it does not help?

## Pain After Birth

Back pain does not always end at delivery. Carrying, feeding and lifting a newborn all load your back, and your core and pelvis are still recovering.

Keep moving, rebuild strength gradually, and get cleared by your provider before returning to heavy training. If pain lingers, a plan built around your function usually works better than waiting. Our guide to preventing low back pain and our page on low back pain are useful starting points.

## Frequently asked questions

### What helps back pain during pregnancy fast?

Position changes, a warm pack, a pillow behind your lower back and a short walk often give quick relief. Lasting change usually comes from regular movement and strengthening. If pain is severe or comes with bleeding, contractions or fluid leakage, call your provider.

### What is the best sleeping position for pregnancy back pain?

ACOG advises sleeping on your side later in pregnancy, with a small pillow behind your lower back. A pillow between your knees can also reduce hip and pelvic strain.

### Is it safe to see a chiropractor while pregnant?

Healthline says chiropractic care is believed to be safe and recommends getting your doctor's approval first. Research is limited, with evidence described as inconclusive but favorable. Choose a provider with prenatal experience who will coordinate with your ob-gyn.

### Is back pain a sign of preterm labor?

It can be. Preterm labor back pain is usually low, may come in waves and does not ease when you change position. Contractions, fluid leakage, bleeding or pelvic pain make it more concerning, so call your provider right away.

### Which exercises should I avoid for pregnancy back pain?

Avoid anything that causes pain, and avoid exercises that require lying flat on your back later in pregnancy. Talk with your ob-gyn before starting or changing a routine.
