## Verify the License in Kansas

This step takes a few minutes, and many local "best of" pages skip it. Chiropractors in Kansas are licensed by the Kansas Board of Healing Arts, which also lists [licensing information](https://www.ksbha.ks.gov/forms/licensing) for the profession.

### Use the State Lookup

The Board runs a public license search. Type in the chiropractor's name and confirm the profession is listed as chiropractic.

When you look someone up, check three things:

1. The license is active.
2. The expiration date has not passed.
3. There is no public disciplinary history you are uncomfortable with.

### Know the Renewal Timing

Kansas chiropractic licenses are renewed annually and expire on January 31. If you check in February and a license looks lapsed, ask the clinic about it directly.

Kansas law also limits what chiropractors do. They do not prescribe drugs or perform surgery. A good chiropractor will explain these limits and tell you when another provider is the right call.

## Look for a Real Evaluation at the First Visit

The first visit tells you almost everything. You should leave knowing what is going on, what the plan is and why.

### What a Good First Visit Includes

According to [Clarendon Chiropractic](https://www.clarendonchiro.com/how-to-choose-a-chiropractor/), a first visit typically includes a medical history, a talk about your goals and a physical exam. The exam may cover posture, movement and neurological checks.

That same source notes that an adjustment is not always done on day one. Bring your medications, past diagnoses and any prior imaging.

A strong first visit should also include:

- Time to describe your pain, your work and your training in your own words.
- A movement assessment that shows how you bend, rotate, squat or reach.
- A plan explained in plain terms, with the reasons behind it.

## Match the Approach to Your Problem

"Chiropractor" covers a lot of ground. Ask which tools the clinic uses and which problem each one is meant for.

### What Each Treatment Is For

- **Chiropractic adjustments** aim to improve joint motion and ease stiffness. They are one tool, not the whole plan.
- **Myofascial release therapy** is hands-on work on tight muscle and connective tissue. It is often used when restricted tissue limits how you move.
- **Shockwave therapy** uses acoustic waves, also called ESWT, delivered through a handheld applicator. It is commonly used for stubborn tendon and muscle problems. Move Muscle & Joint uses a STORZ MEDICAL device.
- **Targeted rehab** uses exercises that build strength, control and mobility so the change holds up in daily life.

## Ask About Imaging, Referrals, and Red-Flag Symptoms

A good chiropractor knows when to image, when to refer and when to say no. Ask these questions directly:

- Will I need X-rays, and why or why not?
- When would you refer me to another provider?
- What are the risks of the treatment you recommend?

[Chirorestore](https://chirorestore.com/articles/how-to-choose-a-chiropractor) notes that asking when a chiropractor would refer you elsewhere shows whether they know their limits. A confident answer is a good sign.

### When to See a Doctor First

See a medical doctor promptly, before any chiropractic visit, if you have any of these:

- Numbness or tingling that is spreading or getting worse
- New weakness in a leg or arm
- Fever with back or neck pain
- Loss of bladder or bowel control
- Pain after a serious fall or accident

## Read Reviews the Smart Way

Star ratings alone say little. [Hot Chiropractic](https://hotchiropractic.com/learning-vault/how-to-choose-a-chiropractor/) advises reading several reviews rather than relying on the overall rating.

When you read them, look for:

- Specifics about the exam, the explanation and the plan.
- Comments about time spent with the provider.
- Notes on whether the person felt pushed into extra visits or products.

Treat "best" and "top 10" claims on clinic-owned pages with care. They tell you what a clinic says about itself, not how it handles your case.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I check that a chiropractor is licensed in Kansas?

Use the Kansas Board of Healing Arts public license search. Confirm the license is active, check the expiration date and look for any public disciplinary history. Kansas chiropractic licenses expire January 31 each year and must be renewed annually.

### Will I be adjusted at my first chiropractor visit?

Not necessarily. A good first visit includes a history, a discussion of your goals and a physical exam. An adjustment should not be automatic until the cause of your pain has been evaluated.
